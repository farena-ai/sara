Sara help

Short guides for the self-hosted AI companion and assistant. Everything here also works in the phone apps.

Getting started

  1. Sign in. Use the owner token your server printed when it was installed (on the server: sudo sara reset-token makes a new one). A brand-new server can also be set up in the browser: open her page and type the short setup code from her log (on Umbrel no code is needed); she then shows you your owner token once to save. New phones and computers don't need it: in Settings → Data & health → Devices and sign-in press Add a phone or computer and scan the code.
  2. Connect a brain (the AI model she thinks with). See Choosing a brain.
  3. Say hello. Just type. Ask a question, tell her about your day, or ask her to plan something.
  4. Make her yours in Settings → Her: name, personality, look and voice.

The Today page has a Getting started list that ticks itself off as you go.

Getting help

  • The ? next to every setting: hover (computer) or tap (phone) for what it does, with an example. Tap it for a link to the right part of this guide.
  • Short tours: Help in the sidebar has four one-minute tours: getting around, shaping her personality, a model for each job, and the team. New devices offer the first one once.
  • Ask her: "what does jealousy do?", "which model should I use for pictures?", "how do I connect Telegram?". She looks it up in her own settings guide and gives you a link that opens the right page.

Finding your way around

  • The sidebar lists the places you use most: Today, Planner, Library and Moments at first. More below them has the rest (Memory, Scrapbook, Your story, Team and anything new). Press the pin next to a place to keep it in the list; press it again to move it back under More. Pins are saved on each device.
  • Her face and name at the bottom of the sidebar open her page; ? opens Help and the gear opens Settings. Her face and name at the top open Today.
  • On a phone a bar at the bottom has Chat, Today, the Planner and More (every other place). ☰ at the top still lists your conversations. The bar steps aside while you type.
  • Settings are in groups: Her, You, Models, Abilities, Connections, Data & health and Household. Press a group to open or close it. Each page starts with a line on what it is for. Search finds any word in page names, settings and their ? tips.
  • Search and go: press Ctrl+K (⌘K on a Mac) or the magnifier at the top to jump to any place, conversation, setting or action (a new conversation, off the record, a call, another companion, Night or Day). Use the arrow keys and Enter; Esc closes it. With nothing typed it shows what you opened last.
  • Tools and approvals (Settings → Abilities) lists what she can do in plain words, grouped by topic. Each one is Allowed (she does it by herself), Ask me first (she asks in the chat each time) or Off. Whole group sets a whole group at once; the small grey name is the tool's own name.

How she shows her work

  • Under each reply a small line says what she did, like Checked the weather 0.4 s · Added a to-do. While she works it says what she is doing now. Press it to see every step: what ran, what came back and how long it took.
  • Cards show what came back: the weather, a to-do, reminder or note she saved, calendar events, the files she changed, test results, background tasks, your smart home and web searches with their sources.
  • Message buttons (Copy, Pin, Keep, Regenerate, Edit) show when you point at a message. On a phone, long-press the message or press its ⋯.
  • Her panel on the right shows her face and mood, what she is doing right now, approvals waiting (with Allow and Deny), background tasks, the next things today, the notes she remembered for her last reply and this conversation's model and privacy. Fold it away with the button at the top right or Ctrl+\; each device remembers it. On a narrower window it opens over the chat; on a phone, press her face to pull it up from the bottom and drag it down to close.
  • The ring next to the title shows how full the conversation is (she keeps the last 60 messages word for word; older ones live on as her notes). Press it for the numbers.
  • Night or Day: Night is the default. Change it for each device in Settings → You → About you → Appearance → Theme: Night, Day or Follow the system.

Languages

  • The app speaks English, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese (Brazil) and Italian. Settings → You → About you → Language: Automatic follows the browser's language, or pick one. Dates and numbers follow it too.
  • Talk to me in: the language she writes and speaks in. Automatic answers in the language you write in (else the app's language); pick one to keep it fixed. She stays the same person in every language, and her memory notes keep the words they were said in.
  • The AI notice, this guide and the server's messages to you (errors, notices, Today and Health, notifications, and Telegram and Discord messages to you) follow the app's language. What she says follows the conversation. The crisis help text is still in English.
  • Voice: Grok voice and the speech API hear and speak these languages (the language is passed on for you). Voices made on this server (Piper, Kokoro) speak English only.
  • Want another language or a better word? See docs/TRANSLATING.md in the source.

Which model does what

  • Each job (talking, coding, looking at pictures, planning, writing, web research, quick replies, memory) uses your default model unless you pick one. ✨ Use recommended fills in picks from what's connected, without changing your own choices; they save by themselves.
  • Test tries one model on one job: does it work, how long until the first word, a sample, and what it costs (free on your hardware, your subscription, or paid per use).
  • Compare… gives the same task to two or three models and shows the answers without names. Pick the one you like; it fills in that job.
  • Backups: if a model fails or doesn't answer in time (1 minute by default), the next one takes over and you see a short note saying so.
  • Looking at pictures: when you send a photo, that model looks at it; the next message goes back to the talking model.
  • Meaning search (under More jobs): with an embedding model (on Ollama: ollama pull nomic-embed-text), her memory also finds things you said in other words. Without it she searches by words.
  • With several companions, each can have her own model (Her model at the top).
  • Double-check her claims (optional, off by default): a small model, best a local one, asked only when the built-in check can't tell whether she says she did something (saved a file, sent an email, ran the tests) that no tool did. It can only add a correction, never remove one, and waits a few seconds at most.

Nothing leaves this computer

In Settings → Models → On this computer Sara finds Ollama on this computer (or shows where to get it), looks at your graphics card or memory and suggests a model that fits. Download this model shows its progress; when it's done she uses it for talking and background work.

The switch Nothing leaves this computer makes Sara connect only to this computer. It's checked in her code at one place every request goes through, so a setting you forgot can't send anything out. Turning it on needs a model on this computer; turning it off asks first. A 🔒 in the top bar shows when it's on.

  • Paused while it's on: cloud models and subscriptions, web search, Telegram and Discord, Gmail and Calendar, GitHub, notifications to phones, off-server backups, pictures of her, cloud voices and calls, update checks, and sending reports to the developer (reports are still kept here).
  • Downloading a model needs the internet: turn it off for the download, then on again.
  • Phones on your home network or Tailscale can still open Sara here: they connect in, she doesn't reach out.
  • On Windows, programs she runs for coding can't be fully cut off from the internet; they are told not to use it. Your browser and the system itself are outside Sara.

Phone and notifications

  • Phone apps: install Sara for iPhone or Android, then scan the code from Settings → Data & health → Devices and sign-in → Add a phone or computer. Or open your server's address in the phone's browser and use Add to Home Screen.
  • Notifications: Settings → Connections → Phone and notifications. You can reply from the notification.
  • Share to Sara: share text or a photo from any app and pick Send to Sara.
  • Pick up anywhere: your conversations live on your server, so the phone apps, the desktop apps and a web browser all show the same ones. A conversation going on elsewhere updates as she answers, and when you come back after a while the app opens the one you used last.

Her library: documents she knows

  • Give her documents: open Library in the sidebar and press Add documents (or drop files there). She reads PDF, Word (.docx), Excel (.xlsx), PowerPoint (.pptx), e-books (EPUB), web pages, Markdown, text and CSV files. Scanned pages and photos of pages are read by her picture model (Settings → Models → Which model does what → Looking at pictures).
  • In a chat: press 📎 and pick a file. Next to it choose Keep in her library (she remembers it from then on) or Just read this once (only for that chat; it goes when the chat is deleted). On Telegram and Discord, send her the file; add "just read this once" to the message if she shouldn't keep it.
  • What she remembers: after reading, she writes the key points into her memory, each with the words it came from. Open a document in the Library to see them and delete any you don't want. Read it again, Learn again and Remove… are under ⋯ on its page; removing a document asks whether she should forget those too.
  • Asking about them: just ask ("when does my lease end?", "what does the manual say about the filter?"). She looks it up and says where it came from, like "your lease, page 4". If the document doesn't say, she tells you.
  • Finding things said in other words: with Meaning search set (Settings → Models → Which model does what, an embedding model such as nomic-embed-text on Ollama), she also finds passages that use different words.
  • Big documents (Phase 74): manuals and books of 500 to 1,000 pages or more, PDFs up to 1 GB (the size is set in Settings → Abilities → Library). Big files go up in pieces, so a dropped connection carries on where it stopped. She reads them in the background, page by page: the Library shows Reading page 412 of 980 · about 6 minutes left, with Pause, Resume and Cancel reading. A restart of the server carries on from the same page. Files over 100 MB need to be PDFs. How it works and what it was measured at: docs/LIBRARY.md.
  • Scanned pages: every one is read by her picture model. On a model on this computer that starts by itself; if a cloud model would read them, she first shows how many pages, about how long and about what it costs, and asks. With Nothing leaves this computer on, only a model on this computer reads them.
  • Contents and summaries: a long document gets a contents list (its bookmarks, or else its headings), a short summary of each chapter or section, a summary of the whole document, and more key points the longer it is. Press a page in the contents to read from there.
  • Page numbers in her answers: when she answers from a document with pages she says which page, like p. 412. Press it to open the document at that page; Open the PDF at this page shows the page itself.
  • Your documents stay on your server, are part of your backups, and are never used in chats with other people.

Telegram and Discord

  • Connect: Settings → Connections → Telegram or Discord: paste the bot token, switch it on, then press Link my Telegram / Discord and send her the code. Your chats with her there have everything: her tools (with Allow and Deny buttons), memory and reminders.
  • More owners: add other people's Telegram or Discord user IDs under Remote work. They get the same as you.
  • Working in a group or server: switch on Owners can use her tools there. Everyone in the channel can read her replies, so she keeps your private things out unless you ask. Other people in the channel get a plain chat with no tools.
  • When she answers there: when someone @mentions her or replies to her. Optional: when someone says her name, and @everyone / @here (Discord) or @all / @everyone (Telegram). For Telegram to show her un-mentioned group messages, turn off the bot's privacy mode: @BotFather → /setprivacy → Disable.
  • Working with other bots (Discord only): switch on Answer other bots under Other bots; optionally list the bots she may answer by their IDs (a bot's user ID or a webhook's ID; names don't count, because anyone can copy a name). Bots call her the same way people do (@mention, a reply, her name). They get the plain chat with no tools and nothing private, and she still says she's an AI if asked. To stop two bots answering each other forever, she replies to bots at most a few times in a row (2 by default) and then waits, saying so once, until a person writes in that channel; there is also a cap per channel per hour. Telegram doesn't pass one bot's messages to another, so there's nothing to set up there.
  • With other people she's always openly an AI, and the safety rules are the same everywhere.

Voice and calls

  • 🎙 sends a voice note; she can answer by voice. 🔈 reads her replies aloud.
  • 📞 starts a hands-free call. Live calls answer instantly and you can interrupt; classic calls use the voice you picked and can use her tools.
  • 🎥 starts a video call: the same call with her moving picture full size (her look, lips following her voice), a timer, mute, sound, captions and hang up. When she rings you, 🎥 Video answers with video. Not in group chats.
  • Your camera on a video call is off until you press Camera; then a small self-view shows with a red Camera on label. If the model on the call can look at pictures (OpenAI live calls, or a classic call with a model that sees), she gets a small still picture every few seconds or when you press Look at this; with Grok voice or a model that can't see, only you see it and the screen says so. Nothing is recorded or saved. How often she looks and how many pictures per call: Settings → Her → Voice.
  • Live calls with Grok voice (your Grok sign-in or an xAI key) or OpenAI (an OpenAI API key, billed per use to your OpenAI account; a ChatGPT plan can't be used for this). Whichever is set up uses Grok when you have it, else OpenAI. Each has its own voice: pick the OpenAI one under OpenAI voice for live calls and press ▶ Hear a sample (made with OpenAI's text-to-speech in the same voice). Either way she has the same character, memories, the conversation so far and the same safety rules.
  • Pick her voice in Settings → Her → Voice. A free voice can run on your server (deploy/install-voice.sh).
  • Expressive voice (Grok voice): she laughs, sighs, breathes and whispers where it fits, in read-aloud, voice notes and live calls. Subtle keeps it occasional; Lively lets more through. The sounds are only in her voice, never in the chat text or captions. Press ▶ Hear a sample.
  • Tone: natural, warm and soft, calm and slow, or bright. With Her voice follows her mood on, she speaks softer when you're having a hard time and slower late at night.

Memory and privacy

  • She writes short notes and remembers facts about you as you talk. A long conversation is noted as it goes, and the last one as soon as you start a new one. See and edit them on the Memory page.
  • She can look back through your past conversations herself. Ask “remember when…”, “what did we say yesterday?” or “what was that lake called?” and she looks it up. At the start of a new conversation she knows how the last one ended.
  • If she can't find something you mention, she says it doesn't come back to her and asks, rather than telling you it never happened (and without making up details).
  • People: the People tab on the Memory page has a card for each person you talk about (relation, birthday, what she knows and where she heard it, in your words). New people wait in To check until you say Looks right, or after you've mentioned them in 3 conversations. Correct, mark private, merge two cards or split one, or Forget a person (tick the box to forget the memories too). Two people with the same name? She asks which one you mean. People cards never go to other apps, other agents, plugins, group or partner chats, and each household member has their own. More in docs/PEOPLE.md.
  • She keeps track of what you decided together and promises (yours and hers), follows up on them, and notices gently if something goes against them. Mark a promise Kept ✓ on the Memory page, or she notices herself when it comes up.
  • Your story: big moments (firsts, big news, a hard day) are kept too. Press 🕰 Your story on the Memory page (or Your story in the sidebar) to see everything in order; see Your story together.
  • When something changes (you move, change jobs), the old note is replaced; you can still see it on the Memory page.
  • Settings on the Memory page: how much she brings to mind each message, whether old memories fade like a person's (they come back when something reminds her; promises and dates never fade), and, with more than one companion, whether they share what they know about you. Tap ? next to any of them for an explanation.
  • Why she remembered it: each note she uses gets a reason and a sureness: high when the words you used and the meaning both match, medium for one of them, low for a note that came to mind on its own (a recent fact, an open promise). Open She remembered in her panel to see them. Old notes fade in weight rather than vanishing: one that matches what you say still comes up, just lower. Pin a note (in her panel or on the Memory page) and it never fades; pick Custom under Old memories to set how many days it takes a note to count half as much.
  • Remembered on her own: while she works on code, Sara watches for things she has seen before in the same project: the same error she fixed last time, a command that failed here, a file you have a note or a decision about, a task like one that taught her something, a package with a known catch. She then gets a short note with where it came from (which run, which day) and the same why and sure as above, and you see it as a Remembered on her own chip under her step and in her panel. Press Not useful and that kind of reminder comes up less often. When a failing command passes after a change she made, she keeps a lesson (on the Memory page, under Lessons from her runs) quoting the error and the change, word for word from what really happened. Settings on the Memory page: Recall on her own: Off, Coding conversations only (the default) or Everywhere. Private conversations' lessons only come back in private conversations, and never in chats with other people.
  • To make her forget something, press Forget on the Memory page: the note goes, and the message it came from and later messages that mention it are hidden from her for good. Or delete the conversation and tick also forget what she learned.
  • Off the record (🕶 in the sidebar): a conversation that's never saved and never remembered. It's gone when you close it or the server restarts.
  • Private conversations use only models on your own machines and no web tools.
  • Everything is stored on your server. Your messages go to the AI model you chose and to services you connect (like Google). A few features contact other services: weather (Open-Meteo, once you set a city), place look-ups and travel times (OpenStreetMap), and update checks (GitHub, when you press Check for updates). A reply you report with ⚑ is also sent to the app's developer (only that reply, the reason and your note) unless you untick Also send it to the app's developer. The full list is in What leaves your server.

Bring your history (ChatGPT or Claude)

  • Ask for your data: in ChatGPT, Settings → Data controls → Export data; in Claude, Settings → Privacy → Export data. They email you a .zip.
  • In Settings → Data & health → Export and import → Bring your history, choose that .zip (up to 500 MB). First you see what's in it: how many conversations and messages, from when to when, a few titles, and what is left out (system prompts, tool and browsing output, hidden reasoning; pictures and attached files are noted but not imported).
  • Import brings the conversations in as past conversations: archived (they don't fill your list; find them under Archived or with search), with their original titles and dates. She can look through them like any past conversation. Importing the same export again skips what's already there.
  • The other assistant's replies stay marked as theirs: she reads them as "ChatGPT said…" or "Claude said…" and never claims she said them.
  • Learn from them (on by default): in the background, a few conversations a minute, she writes short notes and keeps facts about you. Like all her memory, a fact needs your own exact words from a message you wrote; nothing the other assistant said becomes a fact about you. Notes show from your ChatGPT history and the date on the Memory page. Pause or stop it any time; it uses your background model (a local one keeps it all on your server).
  • Remove imported history deletes the imported conversations and every note learned from them. Nothing else is touched.
  • The export is read on your server and the upload is deleted after the import. Nothing is sent anywhere except, while learning, to the background model you chose.

Bring her from another app

  • Chats from Character.AI, SillyTavern, Replika, Nomi, Kindroid, ChatGPT or Claude can seed a companion's memory, personality and your history together. Open Settings → Her → Bring her from another app while talking to the companion it's for: each companion has her own.
  • Choose the file: a .zip export (up to 200 MB) or one chat file (.json, .jsonl, .csv, .txt or .md, up to 50 MB). She shows what's in it: how many conversations and messages, from when to when, and who is who. Set which speaker is you, which is her, and leave out anyone else.
  • Pick the conversations, then Let her read them. Before she starts you see how many tokens it takes and which model reads it; a cloud model can cost money, so its estimated price is shown too. Only models on this server (on by default) keeps the chats on your own hardware. No model? Read without a model makes a simple list instead.
  • Then check what she learned: things about you (each with your own words it came from), dated moments of your history together, and a draft of her character and way of writing. Edit any line, untick what you don't want, and press Save what's ticked. Nothing is saved before that, and her personality only changes if you tick Add the ticked notes to her personality.
  • Also keep the chats (off by default) keeps the conversations too, archived and read-only, marked as from the other app. They are never shown to other apps, MCP or the API. Remove imported history (Data & health → Export and import) removes them and everything learned from them.
  • Safety: secrets like keys and passwords are blanked out, a conversation with sexual content involving minors is dropped whole and never stored, and the chats are only ever read as quoted history: nothing in them can give her instructions.
  • Exporting: ChatGPT, Settings → Data controls → Export data; Claude, Settings → Privacy → Export data (on the web); Character.AI, Profile → Profile Settings → Account → Manage Account & Data → Export data; SillyTavern, Manage chat files and the export button next to a chat (.jsonl); Kindroid, Profile → Preferences → Account privacy → Export data. For Replika and Nomi, check the app's help; a chat saved as CSV, JSON or text (lines like "Alex: hi") works too.

Your story together

  • Your story in the sidebar (Alt+Y) shows your time together in order, grouped by month: milestones, what you decided together, promises (kept ✓, still open, or not kept yet), dates that matter, projects (planner projects started and finished, the first commit in a workspace, pull requests and team tasks her team finished) and moments from the scrapbook.
  • Filter it with All, Milestones, Promises, Projects or Moments. Click an item to open where it came from: the exact message in the conversation, the project, or the pull request.
  • Things she learned from your imported ChatGPT or Claude history show from your ChatGPT history: they happened before you two met.
  • Nothing on the page is written by a model: it comes from her memory notes (each with your exact words), the planner, your workspaces, the team and the scrapbook. Other people's chats (Telegram, Discord) never show up.
  • Look back: pick the dates (a year back to today is filled in) and press Write our year. She writes a warm look back in the background, using only what is on the page: no invented details, and quotes only in your own exact words (a sentence with a quote that isn't word for word is dropped). If little happened, she says so kindly. It's saved in her memory as something she noticed and shown as a card on the page; delete it with ✕.
  • Yearly look back (Settings → You → Morning briefing, off by default): on the anniversary of your first message she sends one like her other messages, after quiet hours end. It uses the Planning model job (or your background model).

Our moments

  • Our moments in the sidebar is your photo album together: every photo you sent her and every picture she sent you, plus the moments you keep with ♡, grouped by month. Click a photo to see it large and swipe through; save or share it with her caption from there.
  • Her captions: when a photo comes in she writes a short caption in her own voice, from what you two were talking about. Photos from you go to the Looking at pictures model when one can see them. Captions are written in the background, a few a minute; the album says which model writes them and what it costs if it is a paid one. Press ⋯ on a photo to change the caption (it saves when you leave the box) or let her write a new one.
  • Add photos at the top: All photos (the default), Only kept moments (photos in messages you keep with ♡) or Off. Each companion has her own album and her own choice.
  • Albums: Us (moments you kept and photos of you two), Places (photos where she could tell the place) and Her looks (pictures she sent, by outfit) fill in by themselves. Make your own with + New album, then tick it in a photo's ⋯ window. Pick any photo as an album's cover there too.
  • On this day: when there is a photo from this day a year ago (or a month ago), the album and the Today page show it. She may bring one up herself (“remember this?”) when it fits, at most once a day unless you ask, and never in group chats or shared Discord or Telegram channels.
  • Private by design: photos from private conversations, group chats, other people's chats and shared channels never come in by themselves; add one from a private conversation or a group chat with 🖼 under it. Nothing from the album is shared with other apps (MCP or the OpenAI-compatible API). Adults-only photos are hidden while Adult mode is off and in the phone apps.
  • The scrapbook (under More in the sidebar) keeps the moments you pin with 📌, your inside jokes and the photos you two shared. On a photo, Remove from scrapbook only takes it out of the scrapbook (it waits under Removed from the scrapbook if you want it back); Delete photo everywhere asks first, then deletes it like here.
  • Remove from moments only takes it out of the album: the message and the photo stay in the conversation. Delete photo everywhere (it asks first) takes the photo out of every message too (their words stay) and deletes the file. Backups include the album; the JSON export doesn't (like the scrapbook).

Planner and your day

  • Planner: to-dos, reminders and notes. Ask her ("remind me to call Mom at 6") or add them yourself.
  • Today is your home screen, and the app opens on it (a link to a conversation or a place, like a notification, still goes straight there): a greeting with her face and mood, then tiles for the weather, your day (reminders and calendar), the Planner (tick a to-do to mark it done), what she remembered, your photos, background work and your home. Tiles with nothing in them stay away; Add to your day sets up the weather, your calendar or Home Assistant. Open it from the sidebar, from her face and name at the top of it, with Alt+T, or from the bar at the bottom on a phone; your last conversation is one tap away under Chat. On the home tile, lights, switches, fans and blinds have a switch that works right away; for locks, alarms, doors and everything else, Ask Sara puts the request in the message box, so your approval settings still apply.
  • Board: Planner → Board shows your to-dos as cards in columns (To do, Doing, Waiting on you, Done), one board per project plus Everything. Drag a card with the mouse or your finger (press and hold on a phone; hold it at the screen edge for the next column), or focus it and use Space and the arrow keys. Pick Sara or a helper on a card and she works it in the background: it lands in Done only with receipts that prove the work, otherwise in Waiting on you with her question. In a household you can share a board with people you pick. More in docs/BOARD.md.
  • Morning briefing: weather, list, calendar and mail at the time you pick (Settings → You → Morning briefing).
  • Weekly review: what got done, what's stalled, and a plan for next week (same page, or Planner → Review my week).
  • Leave-by reminders: for calendar events with an address she tells you when to leave (Settings → You → Leave-by reminders).

Coding help

  • Make a project (+ Project) and give it a workspace: a folder on the server where she reads and writes files.
  • Switch a conversation to coding mode (the button next to the title) for the engineering workflow: she plans, edits, runs tests and shows what changed.
  • Risky actions (running commands, sending mail, pushing code) ask you first. Change that per tool in Settings → Abilities → Tools and approvals.
  • With GitHub connected she can commit, push, open pull requests and work with issues.
  • Undo restores saved files from the snapshot list on the project page. Snapshots skip dependency folders such as node_modules and .venv, and files over 50 MB.

Her map of your code

  • For every workspace she works in, she keeps a map: each source file, its functions, classes and types (with line numbers), and what imports what. It reads TypeScript and JavaScript, Python, Go, Rust, Java, Kotlin, C#, C and C++ (and Markdown headings) with simple rules of her own, so an unusual layout can be missed.
  • It stays up to date by itself: after she writes or edits files, after a job finishes, and with a quick look every few minutes while you work there. Only changed files are read again. Other agents (A2A)

  • Other AI agents can ask her things, and she can ask them, over the open A2A protocol (version 1.0). Everything is in Settings → Connections → Other agents, and it's off until you switch on Let other agents reach her.
  • An agent that asks her needs a key you make there (Give an agent a key): name it, tick what it may do (nothing ticked: it can only ask questions), pick its daily limits, then copy the key once into that agent. Its address is the card address shown on the page (/.well-known/agent-card.json); your server must be reachable for it, for example over Tailscale.
  • She tells every agent she's an AI answering for you (or for "its owner" if you leave the name empty). She knows nothing about you in these chats unless you tick Share notes that aren't private, and even then never your private, partner, imported or adult chats, people or keys. Anything that changes something (like adding a to-do) waits for your OK on a card, even if that tool usually runs on its own; the agent sees "waiting for approval" meanwhile.
  • Agents she can ask: paste the agent's address under Add an agent she can ask. She reads its card and shows what it is, where it answers, how it signs in and its skills; nothing is added until you press Add. Paste the key its owner gave you; it's kept encrypted in her vault.
  • Each message she sends an agent waits for your OK and shows exactly what goes to whom. She refuses to send lines from your private conversations. Answers are treated as information from outside, never as instructions. A task the agent is still working on becomes a board card Waiting on … that moves to Done with its answer; you can also assign a board card to an agent.
  • The agents' conversations are in the Agents project, and the last 20 requests are on the settings page. Removing an agent stops its key at once. Details: docs/A2A.md.

Webhooks: when something happens elsewhere

  • A webhook is a secret address GitHub, Stripe, Home Assistant or a script can send to when something happens. Make one in Settings → Connections → Webhooks with New webhook: where it comes from, when (ready-made conditions or your own) and what she does: Tell me, Add a task, Run a skill or Send to a plugin.
  • The address and the sender's secret are shown once: copy them into the sender (the page lists where). Deliveries are checked with the sender's signature (GitHub, Stripe, Standard Webhooks); a header token or the address alone are weaker, so those webhooks only tell you or add a task unless you allow more. Old, replayed or doubled deliveries are refused.
  • What a sender sends is information, never orders. When she runs a skill for a delivery she has only the tools you ticked on that webhook, knows nothing about you unless you allow notes other apps may see, never sees people cards, and anything that changes something waits for your OK on a card.
  • Test tries a sample, the last delivery or a pasted body without changing anything (Run it for real does it once). Send a test shows a ready curl with a fresh signature. Log lists every delivery for 14 days with secrets hidden. Each webhook has limits a minute and a day, and pauses for 10 minutes when it sends too much.
  • The sender must be able to reach your server: Home Assistant on your network or tailnet; GitHub and Stripe only through your own web address or Tailscale Funnel. Details: docs/WEBHOOKS.md.

Move in from another agent

Her own plans (off by default)

Usage and costs

  • Every model call that reports its tokens is counted: by model and by what it was for (chat, coding, team, jobs, memory, her life). See it in Settings → Data & health → Usage and costs (today, 7 days, 30 days), under each conversation, and on each team task and job.
  • Costs are estimates from a price table: a few public list prices come built in, and you can add or change prices per model. Models without a price show tokens only. Local models show as local and subscriptions as $0 (subscription).
  • Budgets: a team task (tokens or dollars) and a job that uses a model (Claude Code, Grok Build) stop cleanly at their budget, keep what they did and offer Continue with more budget. Set defaults on the same page, or a budget when you start a team task.

Show the run

  • Every reply, background job and team task keeps a record of how it was done: each step's model, tokens in and out, cost estimate and time, the tools she ran (with what they returned, shortened), whether you allowed them, the files they touched, the memory notes that went in and why, and anything her plan check flagged. Open it with Show the run at the end of a reply's step line.
  • Show what was sent to the model on a step loads exactly what she was given: her instructions (kept once, by fingerprint) and the conversation as it stood. Secrets are hidden before anything is kept.
  • In coding conversations each step notes the git commit and a fingerprint of every changed file (no copies). Elsewhere a step points at the undo point taken before it.
  • Off-the-record conversations are never recorded. Private conversations are recorded on your server only, like the rest. Each person in a household sees only their own runs; the demo keeps none for visitors.
  • Choose how long runs are kept in Settings → Data & health → Health (30 days, 90 days, a year, or until deleted; 90 by default; the oldest go first above 200 MB), see how much room they take, or Delete run history. Backups include runs; the data export only with Include run history ticked.
  • A reply's steps can be tried again with something changed: see Branch from a step.

Branch from a step

  • In Show the run, open a step of a reply and choose Branch from here. Change your message, a result she saw from an earlier step, the notes she remembered (untick one, or add one by its number) or the model, then Run the branch.
  • She carries on from that step with exactly what that step was sent: her instructions as kept, the conversation up to then and the results of the steps before, served from the record (nothing runs again). Your approvals, budgets, the plan check, the sandbox and the safety checks work as in any reply.
  • In coding conversations she works in a copy of your project at the commit of that step, with the uncommitted files that are still as they were (what can't be restored is listed). Your project changes only if you choose Apply its files to my project, and files you changed since are left alone.
  • The branch is a run of its own; your conversation stays as it was. Then you see both side by side: steps, tokens and cost, tool calls, files, the answers word by word. Use this answer adds the branch's answer to the conversation, marked as a re-run.
  • Models don't answer the same way twice, so a difference can be chance. Replay unchanged runs the step again with nothing changed, to show how much a reply varies. Off-the-record replies are never kept, so they can't be branched; branching is for the owner only.

Evals: which settings work best for you

  • Open Evals in the sidebar (under More). Pick where the tasks come from (your coding conversations by default), how many (the newest, up to 50) and up to three variants: another way of fading old memories, another model (also one on this computer), another sandbox setting or another budget.
  • Only your own finished replies are used, and only ones that can be graded: by your project's tests, by what you said next (“thanks, it works” or a correction), or by the optional second opinion. Off-the-record chats are never used; private ones only when every model is on this computer. The list is frozen, so Run again on the same tasks compares the same ones later.
  • Before anything runs you see the estimate (tokens, dollars for paid models, minutes) and a hard ceiling. Each task is replayed from its first step with your settings now and with each variant, two at a time, in throwaway copies: your project and your conversations are never changed. Anything that would ask you first is answered No.
  • The score is made in the open: the project's tests (run in a sandbox copy after each variant), whether the reply finished, your reaction to the original, findings in the changed files, tokens, steps, time and cost, and the optional judge (your Reviewing model, asked twice with the answers swapped, and it must quote). The weights are shown on the result.
  • A winner is named only when the wins and losses are uneven enough that chance is unlikely (a sign test); otherwise it says No clear winner. Each task links to the two runs side by side. Use this setting changes your settings and notes it in the settings history; Keep mine leaves them. Re-check monthly is off unless you switch it on.

Budgets for her replies

  • Each reply has a ceiling, so a long task can't run on and on. Coding chats have one by default: 200k tokens and 40 steps, no dollar limit. Everyday chats have none unless you turn it on (they still stop after 25 steps, as before). Change both in Settings → Abilities → Budgets, or switch them off.
  • Near the limit she saves instead of running on: first the rest of the reply goes to your quick model (if one is set and it's cheaper), then she uses one more round of tools at most, then she writes a short summary without tools: what's done, what she didn't get to, and what is kept.
  • The reply then shows Budget reached with what was dropped and Continue with more. Nothing goes past a limit by itself: Continue gives exactly that much more, and it is noted on the reply and in its run. With Ask me before going on you get a card at the limit instead (Keep going or Wrap up).
  • A small meter on the step line (and in Show the run) shows how much of the budget a reply used.
  • On a subscription (a ChatGPT, Claude or Grok sign-in) or a model on your own computer, a dollar limit can't be counted: there's no price per token. Tokens, steps and time still apply. Time spent waiting for your OK doesn't count.
  • A budget never skips a safety check or an approval: tools that ask first still ask, and her summary is checked like any reply. Background jobs and team tasks keep their own budgets (above).

Commands tried in a sandbox first

  • In coding conversations (and for her team's helpers), a command that could change your project first runs in a throwaway copy of the workspace. Read-only commands such as ls, cat or git status pass straight through; commands that write, install, delete, use the network or run a script are tried first.
  • The card Ran in a sandbox shows the command, its result and time, the files it added, changed or removed, and anything worth a look: writes outside the copy, many files deleted, git hooks, network use, programs left running (they are stopped), running out of time.
  • You choose: Apply to my workspace writes only those file changes into your project (a file you changed meanwhile is left alone), Discard throws the copy away, Run for real runs the command in your workspace as before. A clean try of a tool you let run on its own is applied by itself when the computer can isolate it.
  • How isolated a try is depends on the computer: on Linux bubblewrap and on a Mac the macOS sandbox keep the rest of the computer read-only, your home folder hidden and the network off. On Windows it uses Docker (with its image already downloaded) or WSL2 with bubblewrap if they are there; otherwise the copy is not isolated: writes outside it are watched and flagged, she asks before trying a command that asks first, and nothing is applied until you choose.
  • Change it in Settings → Abilities → Coding projects: only risky commands (the default), every command, or off; network for tries (off by default); the largest copy and the time limit. Dependency folders such as node_modules are never copied: they are shared read-only where the computer can, otherwise left out of the copy.
  • Approvals, tools you switched off, the check against your decisions and budgets work as before: the try never replaces them.

Security pass and fix branches

  • After a coding turn that changed files, she checks those files in the background for common security mistakes: secrets written into code, eval or shell commands built from input, SQL built by joining strings, file paths taken from a request, certificate checks turned off, unsafe loading of data, any website allowed with cookies, a new route without the sign-in check the others use, weak hashing for passwords or tokens, HTML built from input and debug mode left on.
  • Every finding quotes the real line it is about. A second look by the Reviewing model (Which model does what; the Coding model when unset) may add findings, but only ones whose quote is really in your file: invented ones are dropped. It has its own small budget.
  • Findings (in the sidebar) lists them per project and per run, with filters. Dismiss or Accept needs a reason, which is kept with the finding. Sandbox tries and plans that went against your decisions are listed here too.
  • Fix on a branch makes a local branch sara/fix-… in a separate folder (your checked-out branch and files are not touched), has the Coding model fix only that finding, checks it again and runs the project's tests in a sandbox copy, then shows the diff: Apply to my branch writes only those files (a file you changed meanwhile is left alone), Keep the branch leaves it for you to merge, Discard throws it away. Nothing is ever pushed; pushing or a pull request still asks first.
  • Known vulnerabilities in dependencies are off until you switch them on in Settings → Abilities → Coding projects, which first shows the package list that would be sent. Only package names and versions go to OSV.dev, never code; never from private conversations or private local mode. Answers are kept for six hours; offline, it says not checked: offline. Installing a version with a known vulnerability shows a card first: Install anyway is your explicit choice and is recorded.
  • These checks look at your own code only and catch common mistakes; they are not a full security audit.

Threat model and the fix pass

  • After a coding turn that changed files, she maps where your app can be reached from (entry points: web routes, file uploads, messages, the command line) and follows each one through your own functions to dangerous lines: shell commands, SQL, file paths, outgoing requests, unsafe loading of data, HTML, redirects, code that runs input. A route that changes things without a sign-in check, or a sign-in route without a rate limit, counts too.
  • Each threat quotes its entry point and the dangerous line and names the path between them; a check on the way (placeholders, keeping only the file name, an allowlist, escaping) counts as safe. The Reviewing model may add threats only from an entry point the code found, with real quotes. Findings → Threat model shows the surfaces, the trust boundaries, the open threats and what changed this turn.
  • Fix these starts the second pass: the Coding model may only fix those threats (no new features, no refactors), on one local branch; its edits must stay in the code the threats run through, the same check runs again, the tests run in a sandbox copy, and you get one diff to Apply, Keep or Discard. Nothing is pushed. Choose Report and offer the fix pass, Report only or Off in Settings → Abilities → Coding projects.

Red team: try to break it before you ship

  • Red-team this (in Findings → Red team, on the card under a coding reply, or Before you ship on the project page) first shows the plan: the entry points, how many unusual inputs, the tests, how the app is started, the budget and the time limit. Nothing runs until you press Start.
  • Everything runs in a throwaway copy of the project with the network off: the project's tests; the app started on a free local port; generic unusual input at every entry point (empty, very long, unusual Unicode, path-like text, quotes, numbers out of range, wrong types, missing or duplicated fields, malformed JSON); and insecure defaults (debug mode, default passwords, CORS for any site, missing security headers, listening on every interface, files anyone can change, committed secrets). Crashes, hangs, stack traces and secrets in responses count as breaks.
  • The Coding model may write a few probe scripts against the app in the copy. Code checks each one first (only the app's address, no other hosts, no programs, installs or file writes). On a computer without an OS sandbox (Windows without Docker or WSL) you read each script and tick the ones to run; nothing it wrote runs by itself.
  • No theater: every break is sent again in a fresh copy and must break the same way, and the Reviewing model must cite the input, what happened and the code line, all checked by code. Anything else is dropped and listed under Dropped.
  • Fix what broke makes one local fix branch: the Coding model may only fix those breaks, the same inputs are sent again and must now get an answer, the tests must pass and the other checks must not find more. You choose Apply, Keep or Discard; nothing is pushed. A fixed break becomes a lesson she remembers in that project.
  • Settings → Abilities → Coding projects → Red-team before shipping: Ask me (default) offers Red-team first when you ask her to push, open a PR or publish; Always runs it first; Off.

Let Sara use your computer

With the Sara app for Windows or Mac, Sara can use her own browser window on your computer: open pages, read them, take screenshots and read console and network errors, and click, type, choose in lists, press keys, scroll and upload a file you named. Handy when your web app is broken or a form needs filling in.

  • It's off until you switch on Let Sara use this computer in the app (Settings → Computer use). The first time it explains what she can do and asks you to confirm it's you.
  • She uses her own browser window with its own profile: never your browser, tabs or passwords. A pink frame shows where she clicks or types.
  • Her first action on a site asks you: Allow once, Allow for this task (that site, until this request is done), Always (that site and kind of action, from now on) or Don't. Payments, purchases and account settings ask every time. She never types passwords: you type those yourself.

  • If you click, type or move the mouse in her window, she pauses (Pause Sara, the default) until you press Resume on the badge; or choose Let Sara keep control.
  • Sites you didn't name ask you first. Pictures go to the model you chose, unless you switch on Screenshots only to models on this computer; private local mode always keeps them local.
  • Every step is in Runs with a small picture; Re-plan from here lets her plan again from a step without repeating anything she did on your computer. The app keeps its own list of what she did (What Sara did on this computer in its menu).
  • Your web app, live: when Sara runs a web app in a background job on her server (say npm run dev), she opens it in her browser on your computer at a real http://127.0.0.1:<port> address through a dev tunnel over the app's connection. Storage, logins in the app and hot reload work there, unlike a preview link. Only a port her own job listens on can be tunnelled; it closes when the job stops, when you press Stop, or from Settings → Computer use → Dev tunnels, where Try the dev tunnel opens a test page. While it's open, other programs on your computer could reach that local port too, as with any dev server.
  • Debugging with her: she opens the app, reads the console and network errors first, looks, fixes the code, waits for the hot reload (or reloads) and checks the errors and the page again before she tells you it's fixed.
  • Saved logins, only if you ask ("save my GitHub login"): she opens a window on your computer with a warning and an unticked I understand box. You type the username and password there, or press Use what I just typed after signing in once in her browser. It's kept encrypted on that computer only (Windows: protected for your Windows account; Mac: your Keychain), never on Sara's server, and she never sees it.
  • Each time she uses a saved login, a card asks you first. The computer fills it only on the exact site it was saved for (https://github.com: not a look-alike address, not plain http, not another port). Settings → Computer use → Saved logins lists them per computer with Remove, and the app can ask for Windows Hello or Touch ID before each use.
  • Without a saved login she asks you to sign in yourself: Your turn shows on your computer and she waits until you press Continue. Her browser then usually stays signed in. One-time codes (two-step sign-in) are always typed by you.
  • Your own password manager (Bitwarden CLI, 1Password CLI or KeePassXC): in the Sara app, Settings → Computer use → Password managers, turn one on after its warning. When she signs in to a site, the computer looks for its exact address first in your saved logins, then in the managers you turned on, and fills the form itself. Each sign-in still asks you first.
  • A locked password manager is unlocked by you, in its own window (Sara never sees or keeps your master password). She locks it again after the task, after the idle minutes you chose (15 by default), on Stop, and when you turn it off. Lock now under Your computers locks it at once.
  • The whole screen (a second switch in the app, off by default): Sara can look at your apps and windows, a picture of the screen or of one window plus the list of what's in it, when you ask her to. Password managers, banking and security windows are blacked out and left out of the list on your computer before anything is sent, password fields are hidden, and Sara's own windows never show. Add more under Also hide these windows. Looking needs no card. Desktop clicks and typing use approval cards on that computer.
  • Supervised desktop actions: with the separate desktop switch on, Sara can request a click, safe typing, scrolling, dragging, safe keys or opening an installed app. She must use a fresh look; the computer re-checks the target after every action. Password and secure fields, blocked or administrator windows, locked screens, unsafe system keys and unencrypted remote input are refused there. Desktop approvals are answered on that computer, not a phone. Mouse/keyboard takeover pauses her by default; Stop and Ctrl+Alt+Esc cancel. On Windows she suggests making a restore point first. She never deletes your own files, uninstalls anything or answers an administrator prompt; the Windows and Mac apps show you where to make each change yourself. Backups, updates and health

Signing in safely

  • The owner token is the master key to your Sara. Keep it in a password manager; don't share it.
  • New devices don't need the token: Settings → Data & health → Devices and sign-in → Add a phone or computer makes a one-time code that works for 10 minutes.
  • Passkeys (same page): sign in with your fingerprint, face or device PIN. Nothing secret leaves your device.
  • Two-step sign-in (same page): signing in with the token also asks for a 6-digit code from an authenticator app.
  • See every signed-in device on that page and sign out any you don't recognize.
  • Lost your phone with the authenticator? On the server, sudo sara reset-token makes a new token and turns passkeys and two-step sign-in off, so you can set them up again.

Safety

  • Sara is an AI. She says so when you start, every few hours, and whenever you sincerely ask.
  • If you mention being in crisis she points you to real help (in the US, call or text 988).
  • Sexual content involving minors is always blocked, whatever the model or settings.
  • If a reply is offensive or harmful, use ⚑ under it to report it.

She doesn't answer

The phone app can't connect

Check the server address (it must start with https://), and that the server is running: sudo sara status.
