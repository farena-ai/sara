Sara help

Short guides for the self-hosted AI companion and assistant. Everything here also works in the phone apps.

I just installed it, now what?

Seven small steps, about ten minutes. Each one is also a card under Getting started on Today that ticks itself off.

Prefer to watch? Set up Sara, one minute.

  1. Sign in with the setup code from her log or your owner token, and read the short note about what Sara is.
  2. Tell her your name and pick Company (or Both) when she asks what you will mostly do together.
  3. Keep Sara, pick a ready-made character or make your own. You can change all of it later in Settings → Her.
  4. Connect a brain. She suggests a model for this computer and checks that it answers (Connected ✓). No graphics card? My subscription uses a plan you already pay for. See Choosing a brain.
  5. Say hello. Tap a starter like Plan my day with me, or just type. A short tour follows her first reply.
  6. Make her yours: move the dials in Settings → Her → Name and personality; the line under each dial shows how she will sound. See Shaping your companion.
  7. Then the rest of Getting started: her voice, Sara on your phone, a morning briefing and a backup off this server. Stuck on a page? Open its How do I…? box, or the index of every answer.

I'm here to code

The same setup with Coding picked in step 1. All of it works with a model on your own computer, or with a coding plan you already pay for.

Prefer to watch? Coding with Sara.

  1. Pick Coding in step 1 of the setup. Step 3 suggests a model that is good at code and offers My subscription (Claude Code, Codex or Grok Build) first when you are signed in.
  2. Open a project: + Project in the sidebar, give it a workspace folder and start a conversation in it. She works in that folder with files, a shell and git.
  3. Choose how she runs code in Settings → Coding → Tools and approvals: she asks first, and risky commands can run in a sandbox copy first. See Commands tried in a sandbox first.
  4. Read the line under each reply: it lists what she did (files, commands, tests); tap it for the output. Cards show the files she changed and the test results, and anything risky waits for your Allow.
  5. Add a GitHub token in Settings → Coding → GitHub for issues and pull requests, and let a team of helpers plan, build, test and review.
  6. Use her from your editor or terminal: sara-chat, the VS Code panel, MCP for Claude Code and Cursor, or the OpenAI-compatible address. See In your terminal and editor.
  7. Set a budget in Settings → Coding → Budgets, so a long task stops at a limit you choose. See Budgets for her replies.

Getting started

  1. Sign in. Use the owner token your server printed when it was installed (on the server: sudo sara reset-token makes a new one). A brand-new server can also be set up in the browser: open her page and type the short setup code from her log (on Umbrel no code is needed); she then shows you your owner token once to save. New phones and computers don't need it: in Settings → Data & health → Devices and sign-in press Add a phone or computer and scan the code.
  2. Connect a brain (the AI model she thinks with). See Choosing a brain.
  3. Say hello. Just type. Ask a question, tell her about your day, or ask her to plan something.
  4. Make her yours in Settings → Her: name, personality, look and voice.

Step 1 of the setup asks what you will mostly do with her: Company, Coding or Both. It only changes what you see first (the order of Getting started on Today, the short tour offered after her first reply and the starter chips in an empty chat); every feature stays available. Change it in Settings → You → About you.

The Today page has a Getting started list that ticks itself off as you go, with the next step highlighted. For company it runs from saying hello to her voice, your phone and a morning briefing; for coding from opening a project to tools and approvals, GitHub, your editor and budgets.

Operations

Current server only. HTTP response measurements do not measure model reply latency.

A configured model is not proof of a successful reply. A backup does not prove recovery.

Record totals include finished history. State counts show queued and running work; delivery records can include archived evidence.

RSS and event-loop lag are process-wide, including other spaces and HTTP servers in this process. They are not current-space usage.

Recovering interrupted work

Open Settings → Data & health → Recovery. Interrupted jobs offer Review restart: inspect the original request, copy it, or put it in an empty chat before you choose Send. This restarts the request from the beginning; previous effects may already have happened. Current safety checks and approvals still apply.

Teams stay held after a restart. Review unfinished work before explicitly resuming; completed parts remain complete. A current privacy or safety hold may refuse recovery. Chat goals retain their existing paused/resume flow.

Native Telegram and Discord deliveries distinguish queued, attempted, platform-acknowledged, failed and outcome unknown. Unknown means it may already have arrived: check the destination before sending again. Human resolution stays separate from platform acknowledgment. No recovery screen automatically executes or resends. Unresolved and All history have Load more so older records remain reachable.

Review text is encrypted; attachments are metadata only. Plugin bridges and arbitrary command/browser/mail effects are outside this native delivery ledger. Read Recovery guidance for limits and backup handling.

Getting help

  • The ? next to every setting: hover (computer) or tap (phone) for what it does, with an example. Tap it for a link to the right part of this guide.
  • Short tours: Help in the sidebar has the Companion tour and the Coder tour (six stops each), the full tour (14 stops), shaping her personality, a model for each job, and the team. After her first reply, a new device offers the short tour that fits your choice once.
  • Ask her: "what does jealousy do?", "which model should I use for pictures?", "how do I connect Telegram?". She looks it up in her own settings guide and gives you a link that opens the right page.

Sara on your Mac or Windows computer

There's a Sara app for Mac and for Windows. It's like the browser version, plus:

  • she keeps running next to the clock (the menu bar on a Mac) when you close the window, so her messages, reminders and calls still reach you;
  • notifications when the window isn't in front (click one to open that conversation);
  • a call brings her window to the front;
  • a small quick-ask window over any app with a shortcut (Alt+Shift+S on Windows, ⌥⇧S on a Mac); pick other keys in Settings → This computer or in the tray / menu bar menu;
  • she can start when you sign in to the computer.

Get it from Settings → Devices and sign-in in a web browser on the computer (if the button isn't there, the server hasn't been given the app files yet). Open it, type your server address, then sign it in with a code from Add a phone or computer on a device where you're already signed in. Its own settings are in Settings → This computer.

Until the apps are signed by Apple and Microsoft, the first start shows a warning: on Windows choose More info → Run anyway. (The Mac download is signed and notarized, so it opens without a warning.)

More than one companion

You can have several companions on one server. Add one in Settings → Her → Companions: give a name, pick pronouns, and start from a new character or a copy of one you have. Then switch with ⇄ at the top of the sidebar.

  • Each has her own name, character, look, voice, conversations, memories and diary. A new one shows her initial until you give her a look (Settings → Her → Look).
  • They share your sign-in, devices, keys, models, tools, add-ons and backups. The planner is shared unless you choose “Each companion keeps her own”.
  • When another companion writes or calls, a small notice appears at the top. Tap it to switch to her.
  • Each one tells you she's an AI when you first talk to her, and the same safety rules apply to all of them.
  • Removing a companion takes her out of the app. Her folder stays on the server (companions/.removed-…) in case you change your mind.
  • Telegram and Discord talk to your first companion.

Group chats

With two companions or more, press 👥 Group above your conversations to talk with two to four of them at once. Give the chat a title, pick who's in it, and choose who answers.

  • Whoever fits (the default): a quick helper step picks the one or two whose personality fits your message best.
  • Everyone answers: each companion answers, in order. Take turns: one answer per message, going round. @mention only: only who you name answers, for example @Mia (accents and capitals don't matter); without a name, whoever fits. In every style, naming someone with @ asks her.
  • Banter (0 to 2) lets them trade a few extra replies with each other after answering you. 0 keeps it calm and cheap.
  • Each one answers with her own model, personality, way of talking and memories, and you see her face, name and colour on every reply. Stop ends the whole round.
  • Each companion remembers what was said in the group chat, as she heard it: your words as yours, the others' words as theirs, tagged with the chat's name. Companions who aren't in it learn nothing from it.
  • Each one's AI notice is shown when the chat starts and every 3 hours. If you write something that sounds like a crisis, help numbers show at once and they all answer with care (no banter). Adult content is allowed only when every companion in the chat has Adult mode on.
  • Group chats use read-only tools only (looking things up, past chats, the library); nothing that needs your OK. Coding, photos, calls, regenerate and edit stay in one-to-one chats for now.
  • Group chats are in the web app only for now (not Telegram, Discord or other apps), and other apps (MCP, the API) never see them. Deleting one deletes it for every companion in it.

Your other servers over SSH

  • Add a server in Settings → Coding → Servers over SSH (or ask her: "add my web box, deploy@203.0.113.5"): a name, the address, the port and the user. She reads the server's host key and shows its SHA256 fingerprint. Compare it with the server's own (ssh-keygen -lf /etc/ssh/ssh_host_ed25519_key.pub on the server) and trust it only if they match.
  • With a password: type it once in the password box on that page, never in the chat. She signs in, makes her own SSH key for that server, adds it to ~/.ssh/authorized_keys there and tells you where the key file is. From then on she uses the key and the password is not kept.
  • Saving the password is off by default. Tick it only if you really need it: it is a security risk (anyone who gets into Sara could use it), so it asks you twice, is stored encrypted with her other secrets, and Forget the password removes it with one click.
  • With a key: paste a private key the server already accepts, or give the path of a key file on the Sara server. Keys with a passphrase can't be used.

Skills: how-tos she follows

  • A skill is a how-to for a kind of task: a folder with a SKILL.md (a name, what it does and when to use it, then the steps) and sometimes notes or scripts beside it. Sara uses the open Agent Skills format, so skills from other apps work in Sara and yours work elsewhere.
  • In every chat with you she sees the name and description of each skill that is on, and opens the one that fits; the line under her reply says Used skill. When one fits clearly, its steps are loaded right away. Partner chats, group chats and other apps get no skills.
  • Six come built in: write a skill, review a pull request, write a changelog entry, a weekly review, debug a failing test and plan a trip. Switch any skill on or off for each companion in Settings → Add-ons → Skills, make your own with New skill, or keep one for All my companions.
  • Add a skill takes a .zip, a git address or a folder on the server. Nothing is added until you have read the review card: every file, every script, and warnings such as words that try to change her instructions, data sent elsewhere, or something that looks like a key. A skill added from outside that changes on disk waits for a new review.
  • Scripts in a skill never run by themselves: each run shows the script and asks you (Allow once or Allow for this task), in her workspace sandbox. A skill can't change her approvals, budgets or safety rules, whatever it says.
  • A workspace's own skills (in .agents/skills) are used only after you trust that workspace on the Skills page. Export gives a zip of one skill; Export all a zip of all of yours.
  • Skills she learns: when you ask for the same kind of thing 3 times on 2 days, or she repeats the same coding steps, she asks after her reply whether to keep it as a skill, with a draft (Save it, Edit first, Not now, Never for this). Sara leads a team

  • For bigger jobs, ask her to get the team on it ("get the team to add a dark mode with tests"), or open Team in the sidebar and describe the task. She makes a plan and hands each part to a helper.
  • Helpers each do one job with their own model (from Which model does what) and tools: 🔎 Researcher, 🧑‍💻 Coder, 🧪 Tester, 🧐 Reviewer, ✍️ Writer, 🎨 Designer, and 🤖 Claude Code / Grok Build. They're not companions: no personality, just a role. Add your own under Helpers and limits.
  • She checks the work: a failing test goes back to the coder, a reviewer's changes too, and a helper that claims something its tools didn't do is asked to correct itself. Her report ends with What actually happened: the files, commands and results from the receipts.
  • While it runs: watch each part live, Message… a helper to redirect it, Stop this helper (she goes on with the next part), or Stop the task. Steps that need your OK (like running commands, if you set them to "Ask") wait for you on the Team page.
  • Each task stops at its time or spending limit (30 minutes and about 400k tokens by default) and tells you how far it got. An undo point is made before it starts.

Plugins: new abilities from a folder

  • A plugin is a small program written for Sara: a folder with a sara-plugin.json and its code. It can give her a tool, add a note before her reply, stop or hold a step for your OK, add a card, run on a schedule, or carry a chat app. Add one in Settings → Add-ons → Plugins from a .zip, a git address or a folder on the server.
  • Before anything is installed you read its card: what it can do in plain words (riskiest first), its files and its fingerprint. Installing switches nothing on; you switch it on for each companion. An update that asks for more shows the full card again, with the new parts marked.
  • Each plugin runs in its own process with only what you approved. On Linux with bubblewrap and on macOS it has no network and sees only its own folders; it reaches the internet only through Sara, to the hosts it listed. On Windows the card says Not fenced in: add only plugins whose author you trust.
  • She still follows her rules. A plugin can add a labelled note or a card, or make a step ask you; it can never change her rules, her replies or what you decided, and it never sees private, partner, group or other apps' chats. Evals: which settings work best for you

  • Open Evals in the sidebar (under More). Pick where the tasks come from (your coding conversations by default), how many (the newest, up to 50) and up to three variants: another way of fading old memories, another model (also one on this computer), another sandbox setting or another budget.

Budgets for her replies

Security pass and fix branches

  • After a coding turn that changed files, she checks those files in the background for common security mistakes: secrets written into code, eval or shell commands built from input, SQL built by joining strings, file paths taken from a request, certificate checks turned off, unsafe loading of data, any website allowed with cookies, a new route without the sign-in check the others use, weak hashing for passwords or tokens, HTML built from input and debug mode left on.
  • Every finding quotes the real line it is about. A second look by the Reviewing model (Which model does what; the Coding model when unset) may add findings, but only ones whose quote is really in your file: invented ones are dropped. It has its own small budget.
  • Findings (in the sidebar) lists them per project and per run, with filters. Dismiss or Accept needs a reason, which is kept with the finding. Sandbox tries and plans that went against your decisions are listed here too.
  • Fix on a branch makes a local branch sara/fix-… in a separate folder (your checked-out branch and files are not touched), has the Coding model fix only that finding, checks it again and runs the project's tests in a sandbox copy, then shows the diff: Apply to my branch writes only those files (a file you changed meanwhile is left alone), Keep the branch leaves it for you to merge, Discard throws it away. Nothing is ever pushed; pushing or a pull request still asks first.
  • Known vulnerabilities in dependencies are off until you switch them on in Settings → Coding → Coding projects, which first shows the package list that would be sent. Only package names and versions go to OSV.dev, never code; never from private conversations or private local mode. Answers are kept for six hours; offline, it says not checked: offline. Installing a version with a known vulnerability shows a card first: Install anyway is your explicit choice and is recorded.
  • These checks look at your own code only and catch common mistakes; they are not a full security audit.

Threat model and the fix pass

  • After a coding turn that changed files, she maps where your app can be reached from (entry points: web routes, file uploads, messages, the command line) and follows each one through your own functions to dangerous lines: shell commands, SQL, file paths, outgoing requests, unsafe loading of data, HTML, redirects, code that runs input. A route that changes things without a sign-in check, or a sign-in route without a rate limit, counts too.
  • Each threat quotes its entry point and the dangerous line and names the path between them; a check on the way (placeholders, keeping only the file name, an allowlist, escaping) counts as safe. The Reviewing model may add threats only from an entry point the code found, with real quotes. Findings → Threat model shows the surfaces, the trust boundaries, the open threats and what changed this turn.
  • Fix these starts the second pass: the Coding model may only fix those threats (no new features, no refactors), on one local branch; its edits must stay in the code the threats run through, the same check runs again, the tests run in a sandbox copy, and you get one diff to Apply, Keep or Discard. Nothing is pushed. Choose Report and offer the fix pass, Report only or Off in Settings → Coding → Coding projects.

Red team: try to break it before you ship

  • Red-team this (in Findings → Red team, on the card under a coding reply, or Before you ship on the project page) first shows the plan: the entry points, how many unusual inputs, the tests, how the app is started, the budget and the time limit. Nothing runs until you press Start.
  • Everything runs in a throwaway copy of the project with the network off: the project's tests; the app started on a free local port; generic unusual input at every entry point (empty, very long, unusual Unicode, path-like text, quotes, numbers out of range, wrong types, missing or duplicated fields, malformed JSON); and insecure defaults (debug mode, default passwords, CORS for any site, missing security headers, listening on every interface, files anyone can change, committed secrets). Crashes, hangs, stack traces and secrets in responses count as breaks.
  • The Coding model may write a few probe scripts against the app in the copy. Code checks each one first (only the app's address, no other hosts, no programs, installs or file writes). On a computer without an OS sandbox (Windows without Docker or WSL) you read each script and tick the ones to run; nothing it wrote runs by itself.
  • No theater: every break is sent again in a fresh copy and must break the same way, and the Reviewing model must cite the input, what happened and the code line, all checked by code. Anything else is dropped and listed under Dropped.
  • Fix what broke makes one local fix branch: the Coding model may only fix those breaks, the same inputs are sent again and must now get an answer, the tests must pass and the other checks must not find more.   • Face lock (on by default): every outfit, change and picture she sends keeps her original face, so she never drifts into someone else.
  • Closet: every look she has had. Tap For… on a look to tag the scenarios it's for: work hours, at home, going out, date night, sleep, workout, rain, cold or hot days, Halloween, the winter holidays, or your own (like Beach trip, with dates if it comes back each year).
  • Dress automatically: she changes into the look for what's happening now. She can also change herself when it fits ("we're going out tonight"), and she knows what she's wearing. Weather needs your town in Morning briefing.
  • Her picture's light follows the time of day, and she makes small movements (a head tilt, a glance) while she listens.
  • Adults-only outfits need Adult mode (turned on in a web browser); they're never shown in the phone or desktop apps, and she changes out of them when Adult mode goes off.
  • Character file: save her whole character (name, personality, look, voice) as one file, or load one (Settings → Her → Name and personality).
  • Colours, chat background and layout for each device: Settings → You → About you → Appearance.

Finding things

  • The search box above the conversations looks through everything: conversations, memories, notes, to-dos, the scrapbook and project files.
  • Tags: ⋯ next to a conversation → Tags. Tap a tag under the search box to see only those.
  • Pins: 📌 under a message keeps it at the top of the conversation.
  • Shortcuts: Ctrl/⌘ K search, Alt T Today, Alt P Planner, Ctrl/⌘ , Settings.

Backups, updates and health

  • Backups run every day on the server. Add an off-server copy (a folder or S3-compatible storage like Backblaze B2), encrypted with your passphrase, in Settings → Data & health → Backups.
  • Updates: Settings → Data & health → Updates checks for a new version and installs it. A backup is made first, and if the new version doesn't start, Sara goes back to the one she had by herself. On the server: sudo sara update.
  • Health shows whether everything is working, and why not.
  • Restore a backup on the server: sudo sara restore <file>.

Signing in safely

  • The owner token is the master key to your Sara. Keep it in a password manager; don't share it.
  • New devices don't need the token: Settings → Data & health → Devices and sign-in → Add a phone or computer makes a one-time code that works for 10 minutes.
  • Passkeys (same page): sign in with your fingerprint, face or device PIN. Nothing secret leaves your device.
  • Two-step sign-in (same page): signing in with the token also asks for a 6-digit code from an authenticator app.
  • See every signed-in device on that page and sign out any you don't recognize.
  • Lost your phone with the authenticator? On the server, sudo sara reset-token makes a new token and turns passkeys and two-step sign-in off, so you can set them up again.

Safety

  • Sara is an AI. She says so when you start, every few hours, and whenever you sincerely ask.
  • If you mention being in crisis she points you to real help (in the US, call or text 988).
  • Sexual content involving minors is always blocked, whatever the model or settings.
  • If a reply is offensive or harmful, use ⚑ under it to report it.

Something's wrong

She doesn't answer

Open Settings → Data & health → Health: it checks every model and says what's wrong (wrong key, the computer running Ollama is off, the subscription signed out…).

I can't sign in

On the server run sudo sara reset-token for a new owner token (this signs out every browser; the phone apps sign in again with a new code).

The phone app can't connect

Check the server address (it must start with https://), and that the server is running: sudo sara status.

Reports and the daily check-in

  • To report a problem from inside Sara, open Help → Report a problem (or Suggest something), or type /bug or /idea in the chat. An error notice has a Report this link that fills the error in.
  • You can add diagnostics. They are made on your computer and shown in full first, with a tick for each part; keys, emails and your folder names are hidden. Nothing goes until you press Send.
  • Help → My reports shows what you sent, its status and the team's replies, and you can answer there.
  • Sara also sends an anonymous daily check-in you can switch off: the version, the kind of computer and which features are on, with a random ID. See and change it in Settings → Data & health → Privacy.
